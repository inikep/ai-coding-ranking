- DEEP — TypeScript, Go, Python, JavaScript and Rust (113 tasks). No C or C++.
- FC2 and FCOD — full language distribution not published; both use FrontierCode 2.0. Example tasks include JavaScript and a Kotlin Android SDK.
- FSWE — V2 includes systems tasks involving C (FFmpeg libswscale), alongside tasks such as reimplementing git in Zig; no language-specific aggregate is published.
- TB4 and CURS — full language distribution not published. Terminal-Bench 4.0 is split by area (software, science, ML, operations, hardware, security, media), not by language.
- MC++ source programs — Python, Java, Kotlin and Rust (Code Migration's other columns score the same ports into those languages; its overall score also includes COBOL to Java).

Together the benchmarks cover at least Python, TypeScript, JavaScript, Go, Rust, Java, Kotlin, C and C++. Most of the table therefore measures general software-engineering ability, which carries over between languages, but the order can change by language. In Code Migration, Claude Fable 5.2 leads on ports into C++ (86.1%) although Claude Sonnet 5.5 leads overall (86.9% vs 83.3%); Gemini 4 Argon does better in C++ (75.7%) than overall (70.8%); GLM 5.3 drops from 49.6% into Python to 26.9% into C++, below the smaller GLM 5.3 Flash (35.1%); DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and MiMo-V2.6-Pro are also weaker in C++ than overall. *Methodology*

Quality ratings prioritize large-repository understanding, complex debugging, code review, long-horizon engineering, and C/C++ correctness. SWE-bench, LiveCodeBench, and other SWE evaluations also inform the qualitative assessment where comparable data exists. The main table combines benchmark-provider runs and DeepSWE scores from independent and vendor publications (†). FC2 and FCOD both use FrontierCode 2.0 with different harnesses; FCOD replaces the legacy 1.1 column, so changes in that column are not score gains or losses on the same test. FSWE uses V2 mean@5 across 34 tasks, with five trials and a 20-hour budget per trial. FC2 has a result for all 25 models; FCOD covers 17, ATLS 17, MC++ 12 (plus Hy4 Preview) and SWEEP 10, not counting previous-version results (^). Previous-version results are shown only for the direct predecessor and only where this version has none; they do not change the ratings. CursorBench 4.1 and Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 have no result for any predecessor; MiniMax M3 is not used for MiniMax M3.1 Flash because it is a different tier. SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Pro, SWE-bench Multilingual, Aider Polyglot and LiveCodeBench are not shown: they have results for few or none of these models.

*Recommendation for Percona Server for MySQL*

Top candidates for a controlled head-to-head evaluation are *Claude Fable 5.2, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2 Astra, and Gemini 4 Carbon*. Fable 5.2 is still the strongest model overall and also leads SWE Atlas and ports into C++; compare it at high effort against Opus 5.5 at max effort. GPT-6.2 Astra costs the same as Fable 5.2 and matches it on FrontierCode 2.0, but early reports of stopping early and being hard to steer make it a better candidate for code review than for long autonomous tasks until DeepSWE and FrontierSWE results arrive. Trial Gemini 4 Carbon before its introductory price ends on November 30, and run Gemini 4 Neon next to it: Neon leads CursorBench 4.1 and the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index and costs less than Carbon's regular price, but has no C/C++ or long-horizon result yet. Kimi K3.1 Pro remains worth a trial for long-running terminal and agentic tasks. For high-volume work such as test-failure triage and first-pass code review, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, GPT-6.1 Terra and Claude Haiku 5.5 are the cheaper options. Where C++ quality matters more than price, Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2 / $10) scores 84.4% on ports into C++, far ahead of the cheapest models with a result (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 55.5%, Claude Haiku 5.5 41.2%, GLM 5.3 26.9%). Test them against real InnoDB concurrency issues, replication bugs, crash recovery, memory-safety problems, complex code reviews, and MTR regression tests.
